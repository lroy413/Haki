HAKI

Every other app tracks what you did. This one tracks the state of your will.

A private journal, habit forge, and mental-health instrument built on one borrowed idea: willpower is not infinite, it depletes, it recovers, and almost nobody measures theirs.

Concept v0.1Built for one userLocal-first

The one idea worth stealing

In One Piece, Haki isn't a power you have. It's a capacity you develop — and it runs out.

Luffy gases out. That's the detail every productivity app gets wrong: they model willpower as unlimited and treat you as lazy when it isn't. Streaks reset to zero. Checkboxes stay unchecked and stare at you.

Haki models will as a resource with a level, a burn rate, and a recovery curve. The whole app exists to teach you yours — what fills it, what drains it, and what you reliably do when it's low.

That single reframe is what makes this a mental-health app rather than another habit tracker with a coat of paint on it. Everything below follows from it.

The rule for the theme

Every One Piece term has to earn its place by mapping to a real mechanic. If a name is just a reskinned "streak," cut the name. Scaffolding, not costume.

Structure — the three Haki are the three lenses

Sense. Act. Will.

The app has exactly three rooms, and they map cleanly onto the canon. One is what comes in, one is what goes out, one is what it's all for. Nothing gets built that doesn't live in one of them.

見聞色 Kenbunshoku

Observation

Perceive presence, emotion, intent. At the highest level — see slightly into the future.

  • The LogJournaling, properly. Markdown, tags, backlinks, voice notes with transcription. Evernote-grade capture, no Evernote bloat.
  • Daily ReadFour dials — Energy, Mood, Clarity, Tension. Thirty seconds. This is the heartbeat of the whole app.
  • Inner WeatherGrand Line weather turns with no warning; so does your head. You're not "good" or "bad" — you're squall, clearing by afternoon.
  • Sea Prism LogKairoseki nullifies power. Track your kryptonite: the people, hours, rooms, and thought-loops that take your will away.
  • ForesightKillerAdvanced Observation sees seconds ahead. Having watched you for months, this sees days ahead — and warns you before the slip, not after.
武装色 Busoshoku

Armament

Harden yourself into armor. Also: the only way to strike something you otherwise cannot touch.

  • HardeningKillerHabits to build — with no streak counter. Each habit has a Hardness %. Missing Tuesday cracks the armor; showing up Wednesday starts closing it.
  • The Break ListHabits to quit, tracked by urge, not just failure. Log the craving the moment it lands. Resisting one is a recorded win — most apps only let you record the loss.
  • Strike the IntangibleArmament's defining trick. Vague goal in ("be less anxious"), one concrete strikeable action out. Nothing vague is allowed to sit in this app.
  • GearsFocus sessions with honest costs. Gear 2: 25-minute sprint. Gear 3: 90-minute deep work. Gear 4: maximum intensity — and then the app locks you out for the day.
覇王色 Haoshoku

Conqueror's

One in a million. Cannot be trained, only refined. It isn't effort — it's knowing exactly who you are.

  • The FlagPirates raise a flag to declare what they stand for. Three to five values, in your words. Everything else in the app can be checked against it.
  • Road PoneglyphsFour long-horizon pillars. In canon, four of them triangulate the location of Laugh Tale. Here, progress across all four triangulates on yours.
  • Log PoseA Log Pose points to the next island only, then recalibrates on arrival. That's how goals should work. One next milestone per pillar — never a forty-item list.
  • Eternal PoseThe one goal that never recalibrates. Your single non-negotiable.
  • Setting SailThe Sunday ritual: read the week, check the Flag, name the next island. Everything else in the app is data collection for this.
Connective tissue — what ties the three rooms together

The parts that only work because the other parts exist.

Will Reserve

The number on the home screen, derived from your Daily Read, your sleep, and what you spent today. Explicitly not a score to maximize — a fuel gauge to read.

Haki Exhaustion

If the Reserve stays low for days while your output stays high, the app says so out loud: "You've been running on empty for six days. That isn't discipline, it's debt."

No other productivity app will ever tell you to stop. This one should.

Bounty

Progression that rises for things that are hard for you — not for volume. Brushing your teeth is zero. Making the call you've dodged for three weeks is a lot.

The framing: the world is starting to notice you're dangerous to the person you used to be. Wanted posters at thresholds.

Encounters

A light log of who you saw and how it went — because loneliness is the largest untracked variable in most people's mental health, and it will show up in your correlations immediately.

Honest assessment

Three things here don't exist anywhere else.

Journaling apps exist. Habit apps exist. Mood trackers are everywhere. Being straight with you about what's actually new — because these three are the app, and everything else is table stakes you can borrow from anyone.

1  ·  Will as a depleting resource

With an exhaustion warning that tells you to back off. Every competitor optimizes for more output, forever.

2  ·  Hardness instead of streaks

Streaks are shame machines — one miss zeroes ninety days and you delete the app. Hardness dips and recovers. It's mechanically faithful to Armament Haki and it removes the single worst failure mode in the category.

3  ·  Foresight

Warnings fired before the slip, from your own history. Not AI magic — just correlation over your data, surfaced when it can still change something.

Non-negotiables

Design rules I'd hold you to.

  1. 01No shame mechanics.No red. No "you failed." No zeroed streaks. A miss is data, and the app's tone never changes when you have a bad week.
  2. 02Capture under ten seconds.If logging takes longer than that, it won't happen at 11pm, which is exactly when it matters. One-tap widget for mood. Long-press for a voice note.
  3. 03The app should want you to leave.No feed. No infinite scroll. Time-in-app is a cost, not a metric.
  4. 04Local-first and encrypted.This is your interior life. It lives on your device, works with no internet, and exports to plain Markdown and JSON. Non-negotiable for a mental-health journal.
  5. 05Two speeds.Usable in thirty seconds a day. Rewarding at twenty minutes. Never punishing for choosing the thirty seconds.
  6. 06It's for one person.Ship ugly, ship fast, change it constantly. Don't build a single setting you'd only need if you had users.
Build order — this one is a real sequence

Ship the spine, then live in it.

The ordering matters more than the feature list. The temptation is to build Foresight first because it's the exciting part. It's last, because it's worthless without a year of you in the database.

v0A weekend

The Spine

Daily Read (four dials) + The Log (Markdown entries with tags) + Will Reserve on a home screen. Nothing else. Then use it for three weeks before writing another line of code. You can't design the rest of this app until you've got three weeks of your own data sitting in it — that isn't a delay, it's the requirement.

v1Armament

Make it do something

Hardening habits with the Hardness model, the Break List with urge logging, the Gears timer.

v2Conqueror's

Point it somewhere

The Flag, Log Pose, Road Poneglyphs, and the Setting Sail weekly ritual — the thing that converts six days of logging into an actual decision.

v3Observation

Let it see

Correlations, the Inner Weather chart, the Foresight rules engine. Deliberately last. It needs history to say anything true.

v4Reward

The fun

Bounty, wanted posters, gear transitions, Haki lightning. Pure delight, zero utility, do not build it early.

Anything not on this list is v5.

Stack

What I'd build it on.

Expo (React Native) + SQLite + Drizzle, local-first. Entries stored as Markdown in the database, exportable to real files.

The reasoning is boring but decisive: this app lives on a phone. Check-ins and journaling happen in bed, on a walk, in a parked car. And iOS still can't do reliable notifications or home-screen widgets from a PWA — a habit app without a widget and a nudge is a habit app you stop opening in week three. One codebase gets you a desktop build later through Expo Web if you want one.

The honest alternative: a Vite + React PWA with SQLite in the browser is roughly three times faster to build and iterate on. If what you actually want is the journaling half far more than the nudging half, that's the better call and I'd say so.

Over to you

Three questions before any code.

1

Phone-first or desktop-first?

This one decision changes the stack, the build order, and roughly half the interaction design. Where do you actually picture yourself opening it?

2

How hard do we lean on the theme?

Full commitment — kanji, Den Den Mushi notifications, wanted posters — or a clean, quiet app that only whispers it, so you can open it in a waiting room without explaining yourself?

3

What's actually breaking right now?

The most useful design input available: what's the one thing you'd want this app to have caught last month? Build for that, and the rest of the spec writes itself.
