HAKI

Every other app tracks what you did. This one tracks the state of your will.

A private journal, habit forge, and mental-health instrument built on one borrowed idea: willpower is not infinite, it depletes, it recovers, and almost nobody measures theirs.

Concept v0.3Phone-first · ExpoLocal-firstHaki mode default

Decided

Two things are locked.

Platform Phone-first

Expo (React Native) + expo-sqlite + Drizzle, local-first. Desktop later via Expo Web if it's ever wanted. Costs roughly 3× the iteration speed of a web build — worth it, because the notification taxonomy below is only possible with real native channels.

Theme Haki mode is the app

Not a skin on a neutral app — the default, and the real thing. Plain mode is a one-tap toggle for waiting rooms and screenshares: a label map and an effects kill-switch, nothing more. Build one design system with a mute button, never two.

The brief

"A man's dreams never die."

This is not a general-purpose productivity app and must never be designed like one. It exists to keep one person pointed at their dreams and to catch the specific ways they come off track. What follows is built from real observed failure, not from a feature list.

Failure mode

The cascade

Sleep slipped. Sleep breaks training. A full week of the gym gone.

Notice what actually happened: the gym was never the lever. A normal habit app would show seven red marks on "gym" — useless and shaming at once, because it names the symptom and misses the cause. The failure was upstream, and it was mechanical, not moral.

Mechanic — Keystone & Cascade

One or two habits are marked keystone, and downstream habits are explicitly linked to them. When a keystone destabilizes, the app fires immediately — on the first bad night, not on day three of missed training when the week is already gone.

This moves Foresight's core to v0, and putting all of it at v3 was wrong. Mined patterns still need a year of history. But a declared cascade needs none — the mechanism is already known, so it gets typed in on day one. It ships in the spine.

Failure mode

The comfort backslide

Lofty goals, then a slide once things get comfortable.

This inverts the usual design. Every productivity app is built to detect struggle. This one has to detect coasting — because a green, frictionless week isn't a success signal here. It's the leading indicator of a backslide.

Mechanic — The Calm Belt

Windless water where a ship goes nowhere. When several days pass with no resistance logged and nothing hard attempted, the app says so:

Six days. No resistance logged, nothing hard attempted. Not a storm — a dead calm. This is the water you drift in.

The danger was never the storm. It's the windless week.

Failure mode

The return

A week off is a week off. What decides whether it costs a week or a year is how fast you come back — and no habit app tracks returning, because they're all built around never having left.

Mechanic — The Return is a first-class event

Coming back after a gap gets logged, counted, and weighted more heavily than an ordinary day. Your comeback time becomes a tracked number that gets shorter with practice. Sabaody, then two years, then Sabaody again — the comeback is the arc.

This also settles what the Hardness model is for: it exists to make the return cheap. Zeroed streaks make coming back feel pointless, which is exactly how one missed week becomes three.

Failure mode

Unfinished projects

Distinct from consistency, and it needs its own machinery. Consistency is showing up daily; finishing is closing a loop that stays open for weeks. The same fix doesn't work on both.

The cause is almost never a decision to quit. It's silent drift — you never choose to stop, you just stop, and the project stays technically open forever. So the mechanic has to convert drift into a decision.

Mechanic — An island has an ending

In One Piece you arrive, there's a conflict, it resolves, you leave. The Log Pose does not recalibrate until the island is done.

  • A hard WIP limit. One active island per pillar. You physically cannot start something new while one is open.
  • Finish or abandon — no third option. Abandoning is a real logged event with a reason, always allowed and never shamed. But it must be chosen, not drifted into.
  • Open islands always visible, with days-at-sea on each. Most people who don't finish things have no idea how many loops they're carrying. Showing the count is most of the intervention.
  • Bounty pays for finishing, not starting. This inverts the dopamine of the shiny new project — and it's canon: the bounty raise comes after the arc resolves.

The meta-risk, said plainly: this app is a project. It's exactly the kind of ambitious, exciting project that becomes number thirteen on the unfinished list — and the theming makes it more dangerous, not less, because there's no end to how much of it there is to build. That's the real reason v0 is four screens and the rule is use it for three weeks before writing more code. Not craftsmanship — self-defense. If Haki can't be finished, it has no business telling you to finish anything else.

The resolution

Dreams stay lofty.

Shrink the unit of action, never the dream.

The instinct after a backslide is to shrink the goal. Don't. That's the wrong lesson, and it's the opposite of what you love about the source material.

Eternal Pose — the dream. Never revised, never made "realistic," never scaled down for a bad month.

Log Pose — points at the next island only. One milestone, recalibrating when you arrive.

Lofty goals never caused the backslide. Acting on a lofty goal directly did — because there's no move that touches something that big. Which is precisely what Armament Haki is for: vague and enormous in, one concrete strikeable thing out.

Optional — your call

Inherited Will

"People don't die when they are shot. They die when they are forgotten."

The most One Piece thing possible, and the most personal: a place for the people whose dreams you carry. Who they were, what they wanted, what of it you're carrying forward. It surfaces at Road Poneglyph milestones and at the weekly Setting Sail — never on a schedule, never unprompted.

Chopper carries Hiluluk. Luffy carries Ace. Law carries Corazon. The whole series argues that a dream outlives the person who held it, as long as someone keeps carrying it.

Hard rule if this gets built

Memory is a source, never a stick. Nothing in this app may use a person's memory to push, guilt, or "motivate" — no "they'd be disappointed," no grief on a streak-break screen, nothing that turns loss into a lever. It's there to be drawn from, and only ever on the days you choose to open it.

The one idea worth stealing

In One Piece, Haki isn't a power you have. It's a capacity you develop — and it runs out.

Luffy gases out. That's the detail every productivity app gets wrong: they model willpower as unlimited and treat you as lazy when it isn't. Streaks reset to zero. Checkboxes stay unchecked and stare at you.

Haki models will as a resource with a level, a burn rate, and a recovery curve. The whole app exists to teach you yours — what fills it, what drains it, and what you reliably do when it's low.

That single reframe is what makes this a mental-health app rather than another habit tracker with a coat of paint on it. Everything below follows from it.

The rule for the theme

Every One Piece term has to earn its place by mapping to a real mechanic. If a name is just a reskinned "streak," cut the name. Scaffolding, not costume.

Structure — the three Haki are the three lenses

Sense. Act. Will.

The app has exactly three rooms, and they map cleanly onto the canon. One is what comes in, one is what goes out, one is what it's all for. Nothing gets built that doesn't live in one of them.

見聞色 Kenbunshoku

Observation

Perceive presence, emotion, intent. At the highest level — see slightly into the future.

  • The LogJournaling, properly. Markdown, tags, backlinks, voice notes with transcription. Evernote-grade capture, no Evernote bloat.
  • Daily ReadFour dials — Energy, Mood, Clarity, Tension. Thirty seconds. This is the heartbeat of the whole app.
  • Inner WeatherGrand Line weather turns with no warning; so does your head. You're not "good" or "bad" — you're squall, clearing by afternoon.
  • Sea Prism LogKairoseki nullifies power. Track your kryptonite: the people, hours, rooms, and thought-loops that take your will away.
  • ForesightKillerAdvanced Observation sees seconds ahead. Having watched you for months, this sees days ahead — and warns you before the slip, not after.
武装色 Busoshoku

Armament

Harden yourself into armor. Also: the only way to strike something you otherwise cannot touch.

  • HardeningKillerHabits to build — with no streak counter. Each habit has a Hardness %. Missing Tuesday cracks the armor; showing up Wednesday starts closing it.
  • The Break ListHabits to quit, tracked by urge, not just failure. Log the craving the moment it lands. Resisting one is a recorded win — most apps only let you record the loss.
  • Strike the IntangibleArmament's defining trick. Vague goal in ("be less anxious"), one concrete strikeable action out. Nothing vague is allowed to sit in this app.
  • GearsFocus sessions with honest costs. Gear 2: 25-minute sprint. Gear 3: 90-minute deep work. Gear 4: maximum intensity — and then the app locks you out for the day.
覇王色 Haoshoku

Conqueror's

One in a million. Cannot be trained, only refined. It isn't effort — it's knowing exactly who you are.

  • The FlagPirates raise a flag to declare what they stand for. Three to five values, in your words. Everything else in the app can be checked against it.
  • Road PoneglyphsFour long-horizon pillars. In canon, four of them triangulate the location of Laugh Tale. Here, progress across all four triangulates on yours.
  • Log PoseA Log Pose points to the next island only, then recalibrates on arrival. That's how goals should work. One next milestone per pillar — never a forty-item list.
  • Eternal PoseThe one goal that never recalibrates. Your single non-negotiable.
  • Setting SailThe Sunday ritual: read the week, check the Flag, name the next island. Everything else in the app is data collection for this.
Connective tissue — what ties the three rooms together

The parts that only work because the other parts exist.

Will Reserve

The number on the home screen, derived from your Daily Read, your sleep, and what you spent today. Explicitly not a score to maximize — a fuel gauge to read.

Haki Exhaustion

If the Reserve stays low for days while your output stays high, the app says so out loud: "You've been running on empty for six days. That isn't discipline, it's debt."

No other productivity app will ever tell you to stop. This one should.

Bounty

Progression that rises for things that are hard for you — not for volume. Brushing your teeth is zero. Making the call you've dodged for three weeks is a lot.

The framing: the world is starting to notice you're dangerous to the person you used to be. Wanted posters at thresholds.

Encounters

A light log of who you saw and how it went — because loneliness is the largest untracked variable in most people's mental health, and it will show up in your correlations immediately.

The app loses its power when you do

When your Will Reserve runs low, the app's own Haki stops working.

The lightning doesn't fire. The armor renders dull and grey. Gear transitions stop animating. Nothing is disabled, nothing scolds you — the interface simply runs out of Haki alongside you.

Every other app in this category puts your state in a chart. This one embodies it. You'll feel it before you read a number, which is the entire point.

If you build one thing from this document, build this.

Armor you can see

Hardening

Renders as literal Busoshoku coating. A habit at 20% Hardness is bare skin; at 100% it's full black sheen. Miss a day and a crack shows. Come back and it seals.

Ryuo

The mastery tier. Hold a habit above 90% for thirty days and it starts to flow red. Earned, rare, and obvious across the room.

Conqueror's burst

Black-violet lightning across the whole screen. Fires only on a Road Poneglyph milestone or an Eternal Pose step — maybe four times a year. Weekly, it's worthless. Four times a year, it's electric.

Gear 5

Unlocked by a week where Will Reserve stayed high and output stayed high. Nika's power is joy and freedom — the literal opposite of grinding. So the app's most celebratory state rewards sustainable excellence and never rewards burning yourself down.

The Ship

The Merry grew a spirit because it was cared for.

Your ship is your body and your space — sleep, food, movement, the state of the room you're sitting in. Care for it and it thrives. Neglect it and it visibly degrades.

This is the self-care module, and anyone who's seen the Merry's send-off knows exactly what that mechanic is going to feel like at month six. No wellness app on earth can land that hit. You can, because it's built for one person who already cares.

Honest assessment

Six things here don't exist anywhere else.

Journaling apps exist. Habit apps exist. Mood trackers are everywhere. Being straight with you about what's actually new — because these six are the app, and everything else is table stakes you can borrow from anyone.

Will as a depleting resource

With an exhaustion warning that tells you to back off. Every competitor optimizes for more output, forever.

Hardness instead of streaks

Streaks are shame machines — one miss zeroes ninety days and you delete the app. Hardness dips and recovers, which is what makes the return cheap.

Foresight

Warnings fired before the slip. Declared cascades from day one; mined patterns once there's history.

The Calm Belt

An alarm for coasting. Everything else in the category only knows how to detect struggle — and congratulates you for the frictionless week that's actually the warning sign.

The Return as a tracked skill

Every other app is built around never having left, so none of them can help you on the one day that decides whether a gap costs a week or a year.

Finish-or-abandon

A WIP limit that turns silent drift into a logged decision, and a progression score that pays for completion rather than for starting.

Non-negotiables

Design rules I'd hold you to.

  1. 01No shame mechanics.No red. No "you failed." No zeroed streaks. A miss is data, and the app's tone never changes when you have a bad week.
  2. 02Capture under ten seconds.If logging takes longer than that, it won't happen at 11pm, which is exactly when it matters. One-tap widget for mood. Long-press for a voice note.
  3. 03The app should want you to leave.No feed. No infinite scroll. Time-in-app is a cost, not a metric.
  4. 04Local-first and encrypted.This is your interior life. It lives on your device, works with no internet, and exports to plain Markdown and JSON. Non-negotiable for a mental-health journal.
  5. 05Two speeds.Usable in thirty seconds a day. Rewarding at twenty minutes. Never punishing for choosing the thirty seconds.
  6. 06It's for one person.Ship ugly, ship fast, change it constantly. Don't build a single setting you'd only need if you had users.
  7. 07A green week is not automatically a good week.Never congratulate frictionlessness. The app's job is to notice the dead calm, not to hand out a gold star for it.
  8. 08Memory is a source, never a stick.Nothing here may use a person's memory to push, guilt, or motivate. It's drawn from on the days you choose to open it, and it never appears on a failure screen.
Build order — this one is a real sequence

Ship the spine, then live in it.

The ordering matters more than the feature list. The temptation is to build the whole Haki system first because it's the exciting part — and that temptation is the same one that leaves projects unfinished.

Definition of done for v0 — written before a line of code

You can open the app, log a Daily Read in under thirty seconds, write a journal entry, see your Will Reserve, and get a Den Den Mushi warning the morning after a bad night's sleep.

That's the finish line. Nothing else counts, nothing else gets built, and v0 is done when those five things work — not when it feels impressive.

Stack

How it gets built.

Expo (React Native) + expo-sqlite + Drizzle, local-first. Entries stored as Markdown in the database, exportable to real files.

Check-ins and journaling happen in bed, on a walk, in a parked car — so it's a phone app, and a habit app without a widget and a nudge is one you stop opening in week three. The Den Den Mushi taxonomy is only possible with real native notification channels, which settles it.

Theming architecture: build Haki mode as the real UI from day one. Plain mode is one strings.plain.ts and one effectsEnabled flag. Don't build two design systems — build one with a mute button.
